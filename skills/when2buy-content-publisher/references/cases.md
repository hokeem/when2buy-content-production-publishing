# Historical production cases

These are workflow examples from prior when2buy production. Treat all facts and dates as historical inputs that require fresh verification before reuse.

## Case 1: Intel proposed stock offering

Input facts: Intel announced a proposed `$15B` common-stock offering, with a 30-day underwriter option for up to another `$2.25B`, and listed general corporate purposes, CapEx, and working capital as possible uses.

Working copy:

```text
Intel just announced a proposed $15B common stock offering.

It also gave underwriters a 30-day option to buy up to another $2.25B.
Intel said the proceeds may be used for general corporate purposes, including CapEx and working capital.
```

What worked: immediate company + number lead, concise financing details, visual centered on one dominant figure.

What to improve: add the investor consequence (dilution, capital intensity, or balance-sheet context) only after verifying it; avoid decorative dates that can become wrong.

## Case 2: Intel upsized sale

Input facts: a later production brief described a `$20B` sale priced at `$95` per share.

Working angle: connect the financing scale to the capital intensity of the AI and semiconductor race.

Lesson: when a story updates, make the changed number the first line and explicitly distinguish the new filing or pricing from the earlier proposal. Never publish the old and new figures together without explaining the sequence.

## Case 3: Riot and Anthropic

Input facts: a production brief described a `$9.1B`, 20-year AI-compute agreement involving Riot and Anthropic.

Working angle: a former bitcoin-mining narrative turning into AI infrastructure.

Lesson: transformation stories perform when the old identity and new identity fit in one contrast. Verify contract value, duration, counterparties, capacity, conditions, and company disclosure before publishing.

## Case 4: U.S. macro calendar

Working structure:

```text
Wed: CPI
Thu: PPI + Jobless Claims
Fri: Retail Sales + Michigan Sentiment

If inflation cools and demand holds up, stocks get breathing room.
If price pressure stays sticky, rate-cut hopes could fade fast.
```

What worked: scannable calendar, clear two-scenario frame, strong save value.

What to improve: verify release dates, times, reference months, and timezone on every run. Do not put a future date on an image based on an old template.

## Case 5: X-native comment tone (not a post template)

Historical tone example:

```text
damn 😂 $100M to fight a one-time tax tells you the expected bill is way bigger 😓
```

Lesson: short, conversational reactions can feel human, but the publishing workflow should not manufacture profanity or copy a popular reply by default. For account posts, prefer a sharp factual observation over forced slang.

## Reusable quality checklist

- Is the key fact new enough to matter now?
- Can every number be traced to a source?
- Does the first line work without the image?
- Does the image communicate one fact in under two seconds?
- Is the wording recognizably original?
- Is the investor implication explicit but not overstated?
- Has the agent checked the account's recent posts for duplication?
